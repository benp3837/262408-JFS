# How to Have a Successful Interview
How does one have a successful interview?  
Let's hash it out together in this ever-growing document

---

## Answering Questions

Obviously the most important part is to know what you're talking about. If you can already talk confidently about what we've learned, you don't need to be too nervous about your knowledge. You can focus on conveying your knowledge and experience.

### What if they ask you something we didn't cover?
"This wasn't covered in Revature training BUT..." and then try your best to have an answer or at least take a stab at answering. If you do know anything about it, you can make that known, but still try to answer based on context and name.

---

### GIVE EXAMPLES!!! PLEASE!!!!!
- **Projects** – "I've used this in my project where we did ______"
  - Lead with projects when possible, don't overdo it
- **Demos** – "My trainer covered this in a demonstration where he did ______"
- **Revature coding challenges** – "We had a code assessment where we had to manipulate Arrays/Strings/Whatever"
- **Etc.** – it's also fine to mention that you saw something in a video, at a seminar, in school, anything that works to prove that you're growing.

---

### Explain the big picture before little details.

**BAD:** "yeah uhhh I set up a Spring MVC Controller with these annotations and it had a method that gets user by id, which talked to the Repo layer in a function that takes a number that should be a valid ID and then shows the user's data..."

**GOOD:** "In my most recent project at Revature I used a Spring Boot backend that processed HTTP requests with Spring MVC in the Controller, and I used Spring Data JPA to help map my model classes into DB tables, and build out my DAO Interfaces"

THEN you can get into individual functionalities.

---

### Ideally — "What, Why, How"
What is this thing, why do we use it, how do I implement it.

---

### Speak with Confidence
Try to leave out uncertain language as much as possible. Answer confidently without rambling! Convince the interviewer that you're a good investment.

- Leave out the "I think... my understanding is... " fillers
- Don't end your answers with "but I haven't used it in a while.. But I'm not sure"
- You CAN say things like "I'm blanking on this one specific thing" if the rest of your answer is solid — just try to describe that "thing" as well as you can

---

### Clarification is OK
Feel free to ask the interviewer to repeat themselves, or clarify, or ask for a second to think. You can also ask them to type certain things in chat if you truly don't understand. Don't do this every single time obviously, but you want to make sure you understand the question being asked.

> Don't repeat the mistake of my associate who thought the interviewer said "Spring Bins" instead of "Spring Beans". So he essentially told the interviewer he didn't know what a Spring Bean was — BAD! (For my non-Java people, beans are THE core concept of Spring Framework)

## Carrying Yourself

### ✅ Dos
- **EXUDE CONFIDENCE.**
- Greet your interviewer, ask how they're doing
- Monitor the tone of your voice! Don't speak in too much of a monotone! Try to inject some energy into your speech. Convey enthusiasm even if you don't wanna be there.
- Yes, you can mention there are nerves, but your word choice should reflect confidence in your abilities.
- Eye contact — look into your camera
  - If you have a setup where you can't look into your camera, just make that known and let them know you're looking at your main screen.
- Posture. Don't sit like a shrimp!!! Sit up, stand up if you can (standing desks rise up!)
- Speak directly and with intent. Try not to beat around the bush. Your interviewer is trying to determine your personality and skill set. Make that skill set very clear to them.
- **SMILE!** It doesn't have to be huge but give off at least a neutral-friendly demeanor.
  - Enter your interview, smile when you greet them
  - Before answering a question, try to look at least neutral. Don't look like you're in pain even if you are.
- Feel free to gesticulate. Talk with your hands if it's natural. Just don't be distracting.
- **THANK YOUR INTERVIEWER WHEN YOU'RE DONE!** Just a simple "thank you for your time" goes a long way for the effort.
- Make sure your space is clean, or just throw on a background. If you must be somewhere that's visibly dirty, acknowledge it. "Sorry my room is a mess, I have quadruplets" etc.

---

### ❌ Don'ts
- Do not vape on cam or do anything your grandmother wouldn't like to see.
  - I know this should go without saying. Or I thought it did.
- Don't be gross. If you need to cough or sneeze, just move off camera or cover your mouth. Try not to pick at your face or pull your hair etc. Yes it's fine to scratch yourself.
- Try not to show any "negative" mindsets in the interview. This includes:
  - Sighing before answering questions "*SIGHHH* well..."
  - Don't yawn or seem uninterested/unmotivated. Your interviewer wants to see drive.
  - Try not to show fear etc. They can smell fear.
- **DO NOT CHEAT. DON'T DO IT. DO NOT. CHEAT.** If they catch you, you'll just get released period.
- Don't ask your interviewer about things they won't know. They usually won't know where/when questions, or the exact role you'll be put on. They CAN probably answer things like "What can I expect after this? Do you have an idea of when feedback will come out? What can I improve on? What does your typical day look like?"
- Try avoiding too much informal talk and slang. "Imma" → "I'm going to"
  - Your interviewers are probably older. Likely senior or lead devs. They're probably not in their 20's. They have no idea about the latest TikTok trends.
  - "Sarcasm doesn't land on Indians" — Vishnu 2024
- **NO CURSING!**
- Basically, don't do anything you wouldn't do during QC.

## Mentally Preparing Yourself

- **BREATHE.** Some of you aren't breathing as you read this. Relax your body. Lower your shoulders, unclench whatever's clenched.
  - 7-4-7 — breathe in 7 seconds, hold it for 4, breathe out for 7 seconds, repeat.
  - "4 in, 4 out, 4 in, 8 out" — Justin 2024
- Make sure you get enough sleep the night before. Try for AT LEAST 6 hours, 8 ideally. You may not realize it, but you do yourself a big favor in terms of lucidity and clarity in your speech.
- Eat something. Your brain needs energy to burn through.
- Know the content from Study Guides and the Playbook if we have one. Make sure you have a reliable answer for each question.
- **TAKE BREAKS!** Have a game running in the background, have a show you can tune into for 5 minutes at a time. Go on a walk.
- Try to study in different ways while doing something mindless. Watch someone talk about your interview topics while you fold laundry.
- **Superhero Poses** — poses that "trick" your brain into feeling a bit more confident. Placebo or not, it works. (I do think it's a little funny)
  - Standing straight up with hands on hips.
  - Standing and leaning with your palms on the desk like you're in an intense business meeting.
- Stretching helps a lot. Stretching releases "feel good chemicals" and it's good for you. If you don't know how to spend that last 5 minutes before the interview, just stretch it out and refer to the first bullet in this section.
- Know and accept that not everyone is getting selected. The Client is NOT your make or break, it's just the interview that's first in line after training. There is still a life to live after these interviews.
