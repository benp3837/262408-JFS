The synthesis is nearly complete. Fourteen months of work, three relocations, and more than a few singularly unpleasant conversations with university ethics boards — all of it leading to this moment. What they called “irresponsible” I call visionary. What they called “a significant departure from peer-reviewed methodology” I call creative independence. The creature in this tank is unlike anything the natural world has produced, and that is precisely the point. Nature is slow, conservative, and frankly unimaginative. I am none of those things.

I should document the key modifications before I forget. The skeletal density has been increased by roughly 340% through a mineralization process I developed entirely by accident while trying to make a better calcium supplement — serendipity is the silent co-author of all great science. The visual processing centers have been expanded and rerouted, giving the subject a sensory range that would make any ophthalmologist weep with professional envy. And the musculature — I won’t go into detail here because these notes may one day be subpoenaed — but suffice it to say the locomotion tests were deeply satisfying.

The colleagues who doubted me have, to a one, stopped returning my messages. I choose to interpret this as awe. The grant committee that called my proposal “ethically incoherent and probably illegal in seven jurisdictions” will one day teach this work in their classrooms, assuming their classrooms are still standing. I bear them no ill will. I bear almost no one ill will. That has always been my greatest professional weakness.

Tonight I run the full activation sequence. The generator is fueled, the containment protocols are in place, and I have alerted my nearest neighbor that there may be some unusual sounds coming from the property — I told him it was a new HVAC system. He seemed satisfied with this. Wonderful man. I do hope things work out for him.
